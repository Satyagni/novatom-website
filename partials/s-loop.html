One method, five stages.

The same cycle runs in any experimental science. We own both halves: the software that decides and the lab that runs the experiment.

  1. State the objective, the constraints and the success threshold. Every later stage is bounded by this specification.

  2. A language model proposes falsifiable hypotheses and compiles each into a formal, machine-executable experiment specification.

  3. Fast surrogate models and simulations score thousands of candidates in parallel. Every prediction carries a calibrated uncertainty.

  4. The shortlist goes to a lab we own and operate: a robot-run bench that makes each candidate and measures it. Partner labs run the same specification while ours comes online. Every measurement returns with its raw data and provenance.

  5. Measurements flow back and the models retrain on what the bench actually found. The system reports a ranked list with confidence scores and full provenance, then starts the next cycle.
